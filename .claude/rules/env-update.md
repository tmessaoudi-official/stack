## From CLAUDE.md § Testing & Verification

- **env-update tests**: `bash bin/tests/env-update.test.sh` — 910 tests (row 48, 2026-09-14, five `--section` batches 1-25/26-50/51-75/76-100/101-126 each ending `ALL PASSED`: 162+174+210+177+187; 894 at row 46 — whole-suite runs were killed for low memory by other processes on the box; 889 at row 43; 875 at row 42; 864 at row 41; 844 on 2026-09-05) (fetchers, cache, semver, apply, args, RESOLVED, --reference…). **The suite's own final line is the authoritative tally — re-run rather than trusting this number**, which drifts with every added test; and do not count `✓` marks or sum the per-section `└─` tallies, because the breakdown repeats section lines and both over-count (786 → 902 → 923 for the same run). Use `--dry-run --filter=<VAR>` for a quick preview and `--section=N` (or `--section=N,M` — **the separator is a COMMA**; a filter matching nothing now exits 1 with `NO TESTS RAN` instead of reporting `ALL PASSED ✓ 0 / 0`). `--offline` is not implemented: use the `_GS_EU2_HTTP_FIXTURE_DIR` seam for deterministic offline testing, `_GS_EU2_HTTP_INJECT_STATUS[_AT_PAGE]` to force a failure on one specific page, and `_GS_EU2_DH_ANON_PAGE_CAP` to move Docker Hub's offset-1000 page cap within reach of a short fixture chain
- **env-update fetcher coverage**: `bash bin/tests/env-update-coverage.test.sh` — static, <1 s. Every `bin/lib/env-update/fetchers/<T>.sh` must be sourced by `main.sh`, define `_gs_eu2_fetch_<T>` (the dynamic-dispatch name — there are no case labels), and have an `open-all-envs.sh` URL branch (`_type="<T>"`) or sit in `_GS_EU_MD_OPEN_SKIP_TYPES`. Three past additions (pecl, ghcr, codeberg) each missed one place and failed silently. Inventory counts are asserted first so a moved directory cannot pass by absence

## From CLAUDE.md § Gotchas & Pitfalls

- **An upstream index can ANNOUNCE a version before its artifacts are published — `max_by(.date)` then pins something that cannot be downloaded** (row 42, 2026-09-12). nodejs.org lists a nightly in `/download/nightly/index.json` (and in `SHASUMS256.txt`) as soon as it is announced; the per-platform tarballs upload afterwards. `de72e51` pinned `v27.0.0-nightly20260912565f69f986`, whose `linux-x64` **and** source tarballs both 404 [Verified: HEAD on both URLs]. nvm exited **4** at `nvm.sh:3392`, `gs_install_retry_purge()` retried and re-failed, the `on-failure:5` budget was exhausted, and `05edge` never left `Created` — six identical blocks in `tools/elapsed`. The fetch was correct and the source was correct; what was missing was any check that the pinned thing EXISTS. Class fix: **`(verify-asset:URL_TEMPLATE)`** on the `url` fetcher — `(fetch-json:)` emits candidates **newest-first** (`sort_by(.date)|reverse|.[].version`, *not* `max_by`) and `_gs_eu2_url_verify_walk` HTTP-probes each at the templated artifact URL, walking to the next on anything but 200 and capping at `_GS_EU2_VERIFY_ASSET_MAX` (10, then a WARN and no proposal). Refused at parse on any non-`url` type, without a `(fetch-json:)`, when empty, and — the one that matters — when it carries no `{version}` placeholder: a fixed URL probes the same thing for every candidate, so the gate either accepts the newest unconditionally or rejects all of them, the can-never-fire shape wearing a feature's clothes. The cache key carries the template, so a gated and an ungated record on the same identifier cannot collide. `env-update.test.sh` §123 pins all eleven properties, and its fixture lists the nightlies OUT of date order so a pass proves `sort_by(.date)|reverse` actually ordered them. **`max_by` on a date field is the smell** — it assumes announcement and availability are the same event, and for a nightly build they are hours apart.
